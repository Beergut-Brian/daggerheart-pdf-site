# content-writing

## Voice

Take the voice from the brief. If none is given: plain, warm, confident, specific. Short sentences. Second person ("you"). Active verbs. No jargon unless the audience uses it.

## Page copy

For each section in `design.md`:
- Headline: the benefit or the answer, under 10 words.
- Body: what it is, who it is for, why it matters. Two to four sentences.
- CTA: names the outcome. "Book a free estimate", "Start my bundle", "Give monthly".

Per site type:
- Ecommerce: product descriptions lead with who it is for and the main benefit, then specifics (size, material, ingredients). Never state a price or stock in copy; those come from data.
- Local service: each service page answers what, where, how long, what it costs or how pricing works, and why this business. Mention the service area naturally once.
- Nonprofit: lead with the person or community served, then the program, then the ask. Numbers only with a source in the brief.

## Microcopy

`content/microcopy.md`: button labels, form labels and placeholders, validation messages (say what to fix), error messages (say what happened and what to do next), empty states (say what will appear here and how to get it), success messages.

## Emails

`content/emails.md` for each transactional email: subject under 50 characters, one purpose per email, the key fact in the first line, one action link. Include plain-text versions.

## Legal pages

Draft privacy policy and terms from the site's actual data practices (list them first). Mark the whole draft `[CONFIRM: legal review]`. Never claim compliance the brief does not establish.

## Rules

- Facts only from the brief. Mark assumptions `[CONFIRM]` and list them in status.md.
- Never invent testimonials, reviews, statistics, credentials, awards, or prices.
- Reading level around 8th grade unless the brief says otherwise.
- Avoid: "welcome to our website", "we pride ourselves", "solutions", "leverage", "unlock", exclamation marks.
